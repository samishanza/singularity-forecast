Method

1. The time-horizon trend

Let h(t) be the frontier 50% time horizon. Its doubling time at 1 January 2026 is D, and each further doubling takes g times as long as the one before (g < 1: superexponential; g = 1: exponential; g > 1: subexponential). Gaining n doublings then takes τ(n) = D(1 − gⁿ)/(1 − g), so

log₂ h(t) = y₀ + ln(1 − (1 − g)(t − 2026)/D) / ln g

For g < 1 the horizon diverges in finite time. The prior on g mixes the three regimes (40% / 45% / 15%). Observations get a Student-t likelihood (5 degrees of freedom) whose scale combines each measurement's own error with extra frontier scatter. The posterior is computed by importance sampling refined with population Monte Carlo, and reports its effective sample size.

2. Fresher data than METR

METR's public measurements lag by months. For models with both a METR horizon and an Epoch Capabilities Index score, we fit log₂ h = a + b·ECI by weighted least squares, then translate newer frontier ECI scores into horizon estimates that carry the map's full predictive uncertainty.

3. From horizon to automated coder

The automated coder (AI-2027's “superhuman coder”) arrives when the horizon crosses an uncertain threshold: lognormal, median 2 work-years, 80% range 1 work-month to 40 work-years. A deployment and cost gap follows (median 4 months). Because no lab has automated its coding yet, the threshold is conditioned to lie above today's horizon.

4. Takeoff

Each later gap is an amount of human-only research time W, crossed while the AI R&D progress multiplier rises from mA to mB. With m(f) = 1 + (mA − 1)·rf and r = (mB − 1)/(mA − 1) over the fraction f of the gap crossed, the calendar time is exactly T = W·(1 − ln(mB/mA)/ln r). Gap sizes follow AI-2027's takeoff forecast. Unlike AI-2027, the multipliers themselves are uncertain (lognormal around their 5×, 25×, 250× and 2,000× point values). The far tail of the last gap is narrowed, because the authors call their own upper bound too high.

5. Updating

A nightly job downloads the data and refits everything. A weekly agent scans lab announcements, arXiv and math databases for capability events and proposes them, with sources, as likelihood ratios. Each event's log ratio is scaled by a confidence level and capped at ln 3 per regime. A human accepts or rejects each one.

Limitations

  • Time horizons measure software tasks. Whether they keep tracking general research ability is an assumption.
  • Training compute keeps growing during takeoff in reality, but this model, like AI-2027's takeoff, leaves that out, which makes takeoff slower than it might be.
  • Backtests show past versions of this kind of model consistently underpredicted progress. The priors were widened in response, but the bias could persist.
  • The takeoff parameters are expert judgment from a small number of forecasters, not data.
